FAQs

Answers to common questions

How far in advance should I book travel?

As soon as possible. November is peak season in Goa and flights fill up fast, so if you haven't booked yet, do it now. International guests should start their e-Visa application today; processing is usually quick, but there's no longer room for a delay. See the Plan Your Trip page for full guidance, including information for extended stays.

When should I arrive and depart?

The first event starts at 3:30 PM on Saturday, November 21st. We'd recommend arriving the day before, Friday the 20th, so you have a chance to relax before the wedding festivities begin. If arriving on the 21st, aim for no later than 12:00 PM.

The celebrations end with the sundowner dance party on the evening of Sunday, November 22nd, which will run late. Please don't book a flight out that night. Plan to fly home on Monday the 23rd or later. See the Plan Your Trip page for flight guidance, and the Schedule for the full run of events.

Which airport should I book my flights to and from?

Goa has two airports. We recommend Mopa (GOX) — it's the newer terminal in North Goa and much closer to the venue. Dabolim (GOI) in South Goa also works, it's just a longer drive.

International guests should route through Mumbai (BOM), Delhi (DEL), Bengaluru (BLR), or Chennai (MAA) for the best onward connections. Full details are on the Plan Your Trip page.

How do I get to and from the airport?

We recommend booking a cab in advance on the GoaMiles app (Android · iOS). It's generally the most cost-effective option, but availability can be limited. Both airports also have prepaid taxi counters where you can book a cab on arrival.

If you'd prefer to pre-book a car, contact Sameer Taxi Cab Service at +91 98502 99989 and ask for an Innova Crysta. See Getting Around for more.

Will there be transport to the venue?

We'll be providing transport between the hotel and the wedding venue for every event, so you don't have to worry about getting there.

What will the weather be like?

November in Goa is lovely. The monsoon has fully passed and you can expect warm, sunny days around 28–32°C (82–90°F), still a little humid, with cool evenings. Light, breathable fabrics are ideal. Sunscreen is a must if you're spending any time outdoors. Bring a hat, sunglasses and anything else you may need to keep cool!

What should I pack for Goa?

Think warm and humid by day, pleasant by night. Pack light, breathable fabrics plus a light layer for the evenings, when it cools down.

Bring along sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses, insect repellent. For the events themselves, bring shoes you can walk on grass and sand in — stilettos and our venue are not friends. Swimwear is a must; you'll want the pool and the sea.

India uses Type C and D plugs at 230V, so bring an adapter. And leave room in your bag for the shopping you will do here.

What's the dress code?

Each event has its own dress code, and they're all listed on the Schedule page — vibrant semi-formal for Mapusa in the Garden, jewel tones for the Aiburo Bhaat, shades of pinks or peaches or whites for the wedding, and anything that sparkles for the sundowner.

That's everything you need to shop or pack. If you'd like a steer on a particular event, or want to check something you've already got, send us a photo and we'll tell you.

I don't have Indian clothes! What do I do?

Don't stress. Western formal wear is welcome at every event. Just follow the colours and the level of formality on the Schedule page.

That said, if you'd like to wear something Indian, which we would recommend for the Wedding Ceremony, it's easy to sort out once you're here. There are lovely boutiques in Calangute, Candolim and Panjim, and if you're transiting through Mumbai or Delhi you'll find even more choice. Arrive a day or two early if you want anything altered.

Are children invited?

We would love for you to relax and enjoy your time with us, fully. So we kindly ask that guests make childcare arrangements in advance. The one exception: nursing infants are of course always welcome. Please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any questions or need help planning around this.

Do I need to download any apps before I arrive?

A few will make your life much easier:

WhatsApp: India runs on Whatsapp. Scooter rentals, drivers, restaurants, your hotel: all of it happens over WhatsApp.
Google Maps: download the offline map for Goa before you fly.
GoaMiles: the most cost-effective way to book airport cabs (Android · iOS).

Should I be worried about women's safety in India?

It's a fair question to ask, and we want to answer it honestly rather than brush it off. Goa is one of the most relaxed and visitor-friendly parts of India, and women travel here solo all the time. The ordinary sense you'd use in any unfamiliar city is genuinely all that's needed.

A few practical things that help: book taxis through GoaMiles or our trusted taxi service, or ask your hotel to arrange one rather than flagging down an unknown car late at night. Keep your hotel's number saved and if you're heading out to explore, go with others where you can. On the wedding days themselves, we'll be providing transport between the hotel and the wedding venue for every event, so you'll never be working out how to get home.

And if anything at all makes you uncomfortable at any point, call us. We would much rather hear from you.

I've got two left feet. Will I be dancing?

Probably, yes. But there's no choreography, no routine to learn, and nothing you need to prepare. Wear shoes you can move in and you'll be fine.

Who should I contact if I have a problem during my stay?

We'll have people on the ground the whole weekend whose entire job is making sure you're looked after: lost luggage, a missed connection, a pharmacy run, anything at all.

Closer to the date we'll share a WhatsApp group and a direct number for our on-site coordinator, so you always have someone to reach.

In the meantime, and at any point, you can always reach us at jayantandanjali@gmail.com. Please don't sit on a problem — we'd rather sort it out for you.

Do you have recommendations for other hotels in Goa?

We do. Our room block at the Hyatt Centric Candolim has closed, but it's still the hotel we'd recommend — walking distance from the venue and the simplest option. Rooms are usually still available to book directly, and if you're stuck, email us and we'll help.

Candolim, Calangute, Baga and Vagator are all 10–20 minutes from the venue and have everything from guest houses to villas. Some of the loveliest homestays aren't on Airbnb at all, so it's worth searching Google Maps near the venue directly. Quality and price vary a lot, so if you're unsure about a place before you book, send it to us and we'll tell you honestly what we think.

Staying on after the wedding? The Plan Your Trip page has our thoughts on North versus South Goa.
